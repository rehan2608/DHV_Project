# National Institutional Ranking Framework (NIRF): Official Methodology & Parameter Reference

**Source Authority:** Ministry of Education (MoE), Government of India  
**Applicability:** NIRF India Rankings (2017–2025 Editions)  
**Document Purpose:** Grounded reference manual for NIRF parameters, sub-parameters, formulas, weightage structures, and ranking methodology.

---

## 1. Overview of the Ranking Framework

The National Institutional Ranking Framework (NIRF) was approved by the Ministry of Human Resource Development (MHRD, now Ministry of Education) in September 2015 to rank higher education institutions in India.

The framework outlines a methodology to rank institutions across various categories and subject domains:
- **General Categories:** Overall, Universities, Colleges, Research Institutions (introduced in 2021).
- **Domain-Specific Disciplines:** Engineering, Management, Pharmacy, Law, Medical, Architecture & Planning, Dental, Agriculture & Allied Sectors, Innovation, Skill University, Open University, State Public University.

---

## 2. The Five Broad Ranking Parameters (Standard Schema)

Institutions in standard categories (Overall, Engineering, University, Management, Pharmacy, Colleges, etc.) are evaluated across five broad parameters, each evaluated out of 100 marks:

1. **Teaching, Learning & Resources (TLR)**
2. **Research and Professional Practice (RPC)**
3. **Graduation Outcomes (GO)**
4. **Outreach and Inclusivity (OI)**
5. **Perception (PR)**

### Overall Weightage Distribution (Standard Category Example: Engineering & Overall)

| Parameter Code | Parameter Name | Engineering / Overall Weightage | University Weightage | Colleges Weightage | Management Weightage |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **TLR** | Teaching, Learning & Resources | 0.30 (30%) | 0.30 (30%) | 0.40 (40%) | 0.30 (30%) |
| **RPC** | Research and Professional Practice | 0.30 (30%) | 0.30 (30%) | 0.15 (15%) | 0.30 (30%) |
| **GO** | Graduation Outcomes | 0.20 (20%) | 0.20 (20%) | 0.25 (25%) | 0.20 (20%) |
| **OI** | Outreach and Inclusivity | 0.10 (10%) | 0.10 (10%) | 0.10 (10%) | 0.10 (10%) |
| **PR** | Perception | 0.10 (10%) | 0.10 (10%) | 0.10 (10%) | 0.10 (10%) |

The composite overall score is calculated as:
$$\text{Score} = w_{\text{TLR}} \times \text{TLR} + w_{\text{RPC}} \times \text{RPC} + w_{\text{GO}} \times \text{GO} + w_{\text{OI}} \times \text{OI} + w_{\text{PR}} \times \text{PR}$$

## 3. Detailed Parameter Breakdown & Sub-Parameters

### 3.1. Teaching, Learning & Resources (TLR) — 100 Marks

TLR assesses the core academic inputs, human resources, faculty capacity, and physical/financial infrastructure dedicated to teaching and learning.

#### Sub-Parameters of TLR:
1. **Student Strength including Doctoral Students (SS) — 20 Marks:**
   - Evaluates total sanctioned student intake across undergraduate (UG), postgraduate (PG), and enrolled PhD scholars.
   - Ratio of enrolled students compared to approved intake capacity.
   - Formula component: $SS = f(N_{\text{enrolled}}, N_{\text{approved}}) + f(N_{\text{phd}})$.

2. **Faculty-Student Ratio with Emphasis on Permanent Faculty (FSR) — 30 Marks:**
   - Evaluates the regular/permanent full-time faculty count against total student strength.
   - Target benchmark is typically 1:15 for Engineering/University, 1:10 for Medical, 1:20 for Colleges.
   - Part-time and visiting faculty have fractional or capped inclusion.

3. **Combined Metric for Faculty with PhD (or equivalent) and Experience (FQE) — 20 Marks:**
   - Assesses faculty academic qualifications (percentage of faculty holding a doctoral degree) and their teaching/industry experience depth ($FQ$ and $FE$).
   - High proportion of doctorate holders with >8-15 years experience maximizes this metric.

4. **Financial Resources and their Utilisation (FRU) — 30 Marks:**
   - Measures annual operational and capital expenditures per student over the preceding three financial years.
   - Operational expenses include laboratory consumables, library resources, maintenance of academic infrastructure, salaries of teaching and technical staff.
   - Capital expenses include creation of new buildings, specialized laboratories, and major equipment acquisitions.

---

### 3.2. Research and Professional Practice (RPC) — 100 Marks

RPC assesses the scholarly output, research quality, intellectual property creation, and industry engagement of the institution.

#### Sub-Parameters of RPC:
1. **Combined Metric for Publications (PU) — 35 Marks:**
   - Measures the total number of peer-reviewed research publications indexed in reputable citation databases (Web of Science and Scopus) over the preceding three calendar years.
   - Normalized relative to the number of faculty members.

2. **Combined Metric for Quality of Publications / Citations (QP) — 35 / 40 Marks:**
   - Measures the citation impact and quality of research output.
   - Evaluates total citations received in the preceding three years for publications published during the preceding three years.
   - Evaluates Top 25% percentile publications (highly cited papers / Q1 journals).
   - Includes $h$-index and citations per paper metrics.

3. **IPR and Patents: Published, Granted and Licensed (IPR) — 15 Marks:**
   - Measures patent activity over the preceding three years:
     - Patents published.
     - Patents granted.
     - Patents licensed and commercialized (technology transfer).

4. **Footprint of Projects and Professional Practice (FPPP) — 15 Marks:**
   - Evaluates sponsored research projects funding received from government and non-government agencies over three years.
   - Evaluates consultancy project earnings and executive development program earnings.

---

### 3.3. Graduation Outcomes (GO) — 100 Marks

GO measures the real-world success of students completing their degrees from the institution.

#### Sub-Parameters of GO:
1. **Metric for University Examinations (GUE) — 50–60 Marks:**
   - Percentage of students graduating in the minimum stipulated time without academic backlogs.
2. **Metric for Median Salary (GMS) — 25–40 Marks:**
   - Median annual salary of placed graduates over the previous three years as reported through verified placement records.
3. **Metric for Graduating Students Admitted to Top Higher Education / PhD (GPHD) — 15–20 Marks:**
   - Number of graduating students admitted to top national and international institutions for master's or doctoral programs, or the total number of PhD students graduated over the previous three years.

---

### 3.4. Outreach and Inclusivity (OI) — 100 Marks

OI measures diversity, inclusivity, and equitable access across geographical, gender, and socio-economic lines.

#### Sub-Parameters of OI:
1. **Percentage of Students from Other States / Countries (RD - Region Diversity) — 30 Marks:**
   - Measures geographical diversity of the student body: percentage of students enrolled from outside the home state and international students.
2. **Percentage of Women (WD - Women Diversity) — 30 Marks:**
   - Proportion of female students and female faculty members across all departments.
3. **Economically and Socially Challenged Students (ESC) — 20 Marks:**
   - Percentage of students receiving full or substantial tuition fee reimbursement from government or institutional scholarships.
4. **Facilities for Physically Challenged Students (PCS) — 20 Marks:**
   - Physical infrastructure for differently-abled students (wheelchair accessibility, ramps, lifts, specialized restrooms, assistive technologies).

---

### 3.5. Perception (PR) — 100 Marks

PR measures institutional reputation among academic peers, employers, and public stakeholders.

#### Sub-Parameters of PR:
1. **Peer Perception: Academic Peers and Employers (PREMP) — 100 Marks:**
   - Survey-based assessment administered to academic peers (deans, professors, directors) and corporate recruiters/employers.
   - Objective is to assess academic standing, graduate capability, and overall brand prestige.

---

## 4. Key Differences: TLR vs. RPC

A common point of inquiry is the fundamental distinction between TLR and RPC:

| Feature / Dimension | Teaching, Learning & Resources (TLR) | Research & Professional Practice (RPC) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Focus** | Educational inputs, internal resources, pedagogy | Research output, scholarly impact, external impact |
| **Key Inputs** | Enrolled student counts, faculty headcount, faculty qualifications | Indexed papers (Scopus/WoS), patents, grants |
| **Financial Factor** | Operational spending on labs, salaries, libraries | Sponsored research revenue and consultancy earnings |
| **Measurement Horizon**| Current academic year + 3-year expenditure average | Cumulative 3-year publication & citation window |
| **Institutional Role** | Assesses how well the institution teaches students | Assesses how actively the institution creates knowledge |

## 5. Research Specific Metric Schema (Introduced 2021)

Starting in 2021, NIRF introduced the dedicated **Research Institutions** category. Unlike the standard 5-parameter schema, this category uses a specialized schema:
1. **Research Scholarly Faculty & Capacity (`research_sfc`):** Focuses heavily on doctoral faculty, dedicated post-doctoral fellows, and research scholar density.
2. **Research Quantitative Output (`research_qnr`):** Focuses on publication volume, journal indexing depth, and faculty research productivity.
3. **Research Qualitative Metrics (`research_qlr`):** Focuses on citation impact, top-decile publications, high-impact journals, and collaborative international publications.
4. **Outreach & Inclusivity (`oi`):** Retained with focused weighting.
5. **Perception (`perception`):** Evaluates specialized research reputation.

## 6. Longitudinal Comparability & Cohort Boundaries

### 6.1. Expanding Ranking Depths
- In 2017, Management was ranked only to Rank 50, later expanding to Top 75 in 2020 and Top 100 in 2025.
- Engineering and Universities expanded from Top 100 to Top 200 (with ranks 101–200 placed in 50-rank bands).
- Colleges expanded from Top 100 to Top 200.

### 6.2. Rank Bands (101–150 and 151–200)
- In NIRF, institutions ranked between 101–150 or 151–200 are reported in alphabetical or arbitrary order within bands.
- Numerical composite scores are deliberately withheld by NIRF for institutions in these bands.

### 6.3. Annual Curve Normalization
- Composite scores and parameter scores are normalized annually relative to the highest-performing institution in that specific year and category.
- Consequently, an absolute TLR score of 72.5 in 2018 is not mathematically identical to 72.5 in 2024; cross-year tracking must account for cohort normalization.
